- Adapted <span class="nb">nnU-Net</span>'s preprocessing and data pipeline so SwinUNETR and <span class="nb">U-Net++</span> could be trained from scratch on identical inputs, matching its per-channel z-score normalisation for a like-for-like comparison.
- Handled 1,500 patients' 3D bi-parametric MRI volumes with Nibabel and NumPy across T2, ADC and DWI.
- Compared a hybrid Vision Transformer against a deeply supervised nested CNN on how each localises and diagnoses lesions.
- Built the Streamlit dashboard around a 3D slice viewer, interactive ROC curves and confusion matrices, so clinical stakeholders can explore the results without reading code.

## Slice viewer

The scan explorer shows the T2, ADC and DWI modalities side by side, with the model's probability heatmap, its predicted lesion boundary and the expert ground-truth mask laid over them. A fourth panel zooms in on the predicted lesion.

- A slider steps through the slices, and the ground-truth and prediction layers can each be switched off.
- An inspector above the images reports the model's maximum confidence for the case and whether the expert label is positive.
- Erosion, dilation and zoom run through SciPy and NumPy, the heatmap colouring through Matplotlib, and the slice animation through Plotly.

## Result

The <span class="nb">nnU-Net</span> ensemble had the best average precision, 0.462 against 0.309 for SwinUNETR and 0.266 for <span class="nb">U-Net++</span>. <span class="nb">U-Net++</span> raised the most false alarms, 4.86 per case against 3.16 for the ensemble. That is the kind of trade-off a single AUROC hides.
